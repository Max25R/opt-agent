## Primary outcome: R1 is supported

R1: among the answers the agent would report, a flagged answer is wrong more often than
an unflagged one. The units are single-shot sample 3, one per problem, where it's
verified: 119 of the 121 problems (the other 2 gave no verified answer). A unit is flagged when
samples 3 to 7 don't all agree or the no-solver baseline's answer differs, and it's a
silent failure when it's wrong. Fisher's exact test, one-sided, on flagged by silent
failure: **p = 2.3e-6, so R1 is supported**
(pre-registered: supported if p is below alpha 0.05).

| unit | silent failure | right |
|---|---:|---:|
| flagged | 20 | 18 |
| not flagged | 9 | 72 |

## R1's effect (reported whatever p is)

With one unit per problem, the units are independent, so the 95% Wilson intervals are
honest.

| | of | share (95% Wilson) |
|---|---|---|
| base rate: silent failures, of the units | 29 of 119 | 24.4% (17.5–32.8%) |
| flag rate: flagged, of the units | 38 of 119 | 31.9% (24.2–40.8%) |
| **precision:** silent failures, of the flagged | 20 of 38 | 52.6% (37.3–67.5%) |
| **recall:** flagged, of the silent failures | 20 of 29 | 69.0% (50.8–82.7%) |

Precision is 2.16 times the base rate.

## R2 (secondary): the majority of five samples against one

On all 121 problems, sample 3 alone was right on 90, and the majority of samples 3 to 7
(an answer at least 3 of the five give, counting verified answers only) on 101:
11 problems only the majority got right, and 0 only the one sample. Exact McNemar, two-sided:
p = 0.00098, **detected**.

## Reported without a test

- **Without the 13 disputed problems** (secondary, no claim; an LLM judge flagged their
  keys, not verified by a person): 106 units, p = 4.0e-6.
  Base rate 15.1% (16 of 106); precision 41.9%
  (13 of 31, 26.4–59.2%); recall 81.2%
  (13 of 16, 57.0–93.4%); precision 2.78 times the base rate.
- **Abstaining on a flag:** withholding the flagged units would make 20 silent
  failures loud and lose 18 right answers; 9 silent failures would still get through.
